What works: peak and breather

The series with the highest pull have both: a peak episode that shocks you, and a breather that builds the characters.

See the explanation: what it is, why I chose it, and how I checked it

What it is. Count the big turning points in each episode. A peak episode has 1.4 or more per minute; a breather has 0.8 or less. In my sample, the three series with the highest average pull have both: a peak shocks you, a breather lets you believe the characters and fear for them. These are working cutoffs that separated the episodes I measured, not a universal rule. In Triplets, the factory and family episodes slow down (0.8), then the bar episode hits: Daisy is drugged in a bar, and Marcus finds her in the factory and saves her.

How to read it. Each dot is one series: across is its strongest episode, up is its calmest. Only the pink corner has both a peak and a breather.

Why I chose it. Genre and the average twist rate didn't explain pull. Doctor Boss, Genie and Final Boss average about 0.8 big twists a minute, yet only Doctor Boss has a high pull score, together with Hockey Star and Triplets. What the three share is rhythm. Queen's Heart is the counterexample: the most big twists of the six (2.0 a minute), but a pull score of only 2.0. The pattern is visible across all six, but six series are a hypothesis, not proof. Prediction: in Shortical's own data, series with both a peak and a breather in their first five episodes should keep more viewers to the paywall than series with only one of the two.

A second signal. Scenes per minute, a completely separate measure, splits the series into the same three groups: 1.6–2.0 for the three highest-pull series, 1.3–1.4 for the two with no peak, 0.9 for Queen's Heart. Both fully AI series sit at the low end (Queen's Heart 0.9, Genie 1.4), even though in AI a new location costs almost nothing. That's an unused, cheap lever to test.

Outside viewers. Two other viewers watched the same three pieces: the Triplets ad, an episode of Doctor Boss and an episode of Queen's Heart. Doctor Boss scored highest (pull 4), Queen's Heart lowest (pull 2), and its acting felt "very fake". The same pattern, from fresh eyes. A small qualitative check, not a sample.

The three groups

Peak and breather: Hockey Star, Doctor Boss, Triplets. A shock episode, then a calmer one that builds the characters. The highest pull scores (2.8–3.0).

No peak: Final Boss, Genie. Steady and slow: no episode above 1.2 big twists a minute. Pull 2.5 and 1.8.

No breather: Queen's Heart. Every episode at 1.7 big twists a minute or more. Constant shocks, the lowest emotion score (1.0), pull 2.0.

What it means for Shortical: plan a peak episode, then a breather; check the rhythm of the first episodes before licensing a series; and in AI productions, add locations, which cost little.
