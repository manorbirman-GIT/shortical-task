Part 2a

The Triplets ad

The linked creative runs about 14 minutes: a long-form ad of early episodes, recut and reordered, ending just before the payoff.

The ad at a glance

Each tick is a big twist: the 10 big twists out of 35 turning points I identified. A big twist changes the story's direction or the balance of power.

The hook · 0:00-0:34

What happens

A hospital bed. 'You're pregnant' (0:04), 'with triplets' (0:05), 'he told me he was sterile' (0:13), and she doesn't know who the father is.

Why it works

Story loops · 1
Seeds

The first 30 seconds plant how, who, and how will she manage.

Story loops · 2
Growth

The ad answers each one: the flashback shows how, the reveal shows who, her family shows what she faces. Smaller loops along the way: is Marcus good, will his family accept her, will he believe her, will she lose the babies?

Story loops · 3
A new question

By the end every opening question is answered, but a bigger one is open: will he save her in time? That is the reason to install.

Why I think this creative won

  • It starts at the result. The first 15 seconds set up the premise, and the viewer stays to find out how.
  • First unclear, then clear good and bad. Marcus is interesting because we don't know him; then the family becomes the clear villain.
  • Contrast editing. His warm, rich home is cut against her standing in the rain, bleeding.
  • Escalating suffering. Each beat is worse than the last. In my test group, the moment that grabbed the viewer was fear for Daisy, not the twists.
  • She doesn't deserve it. She returned the money, so the viewer wants the payoff badly.
  • The rescue starts but doesn't end. Marcus runs to her, and the ad cuts. The only way to find out is to install.
  • It's the selection and the order. The ad is built from the series' own scenes; what makes it work is which ones it picks and the order it puts them in.

Story loops

The opening seeds how, who, and how will she survive. The ad answers how and who, shows what she is up against, and ends on a bigger question. The flaw planted in Act 1 (he thinks she's a gold digger) drives her final decision, so the cliffhanger grows out of the story. It is also not a misleading ad: every scene comes from episodes 1-20, and it promises only what those episodes deliver.

How it applies what worked in Triplets
  • Start at the result: the pregnancy reveal opens the ad.
  • First unclear, then clear good and bad: Graham seems to judge her; her mother becomes the clear villain.
  • Contrast: his world of success against her home of cleaning, violence and money.
  • Escalating suffering: an old man, 'slut', a broom, a hospital bed.
  • She doesn't deserve it: she would rather lose everything than trap him.
  • Show the protector once, hold back the ending: he saves her at the nightclub; the ad stops before the next rescue.
What I kept short or left out
  • The childhood rescue coincidence: one 10-second flashback (1:31-1:41), left as a question. One test viewer said that is where she would stop watching; a second found it intriguing. So I kept it short.
  • The fiancée: she only arrives around episode 20.
  • The second rescue: the broom scene is cut before Graham arrives, so the wish for a rescue keeps building.
  • The hospital storyline: the staff treat her badly at work; left out to keep one clear storyline, and because it only develops after episode 30.

  • Decision rule: run both endings to the same audience; the one with the better conversion wins.
Length

8:14 or 7:42, well under the 14-minute Triplets ad, which felt too long to my test group. I watched about 15 more episodes in the ReelShort app and chose not to add more storylines: my test group said the ad should stay focused. In the real world I would let the data decide (where viewers drop off, which peaks hold them); without per-episode or per-second data, I relied on my test group.
