Part 3

Licensing: which shows to buy

Total views mostly show how old a show is. So I ranked by what a show earns each day, and checked whether its audience pays.

How I ranked

70% of the score
Daily views

Total views divided by days since release (to 15 Sep 2026). Total views mostly show how old a show is. Daily views are an average since launch, not proof that demand is steady.

30% of the score
Platform RPM

The app's revenue per 1,000 views, a term from advertising. It is platform-level (catalogue revenue over the views in this file), so it shows how well a platform earns per view, not what one show earns. Both are turned into ranks from 0 to 100, so views and dollars can be added.

To qualify
The target zone

I wanted the top 10, and that came to about 1M daily views, about twice the median. And I prefer shows from platforms that earn more per view: RPM above $90. Then the score sets the order.

Data check first: Velvet Shadows appears twice (same date, same views), so I counted it once. Northern Gate has a release date (9.10.2026) after the reference date (15.9.2026) but already shows 96.4M views; left out until the date is confirmed. Amber Protocol has no views; left out instead of guessing a number. 122 shows remain.

Part 3b

Crown Fire or Eternal Circle

Decision
Crown Fire

We know it has stronger demand. We don't know that Eternal Circle earns more at show level, so I wouldn't pay for that theoretical advantage.

Money per day: daily views x platform RPM

5.6M views a day at $14 per 1,000 views, against 3.95M at $20. In money per day it is a tie.

Why Crown Fire

  • 42% more daily views, the only number we have at the level of the show itself.
  • On money it is a tie: about $79K a day against $81K. Both sit on the two lowest-RPM platforms, so RPM can't separate them, and demand decides.
  • Demand is the part we can see. On Shortical the show runs through our own paywall; what we know moves with it is the demand.

What we give up

  • 23 more days of proof (89 vs. 66).
  • A platform with a higher historical RPM. Aura earns $20 per 1,000 views against Pulse's $14, but in money per day it is still a tie.

What to track, and when I would switch to Eternal Circle

To limit the risk: a short license with a renewal option, and part of the payment tied to performance. The 40% lines are decision thresholds I chose, close to Crown Fire's 42% lead in daily views.

In a perfect world

When the data doesn't give a clear answer, as here, I would add a standing viewer panel inside the platform, mostly women who match our audience, English- and Chinese-speaking, like test screenings in film. They watch the first episodes of both shows, and their reaction counts for up to 30% of the decision.
